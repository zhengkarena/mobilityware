02 · The Data Spine

Unified attribution architecture

Building the trustworthy backbone before building any chart on top of it.

AThe problem, stated plainly

Eight silos, four install counts, one decision to make

UA data at any modern studio lives in eight-plus silos. Ten ad networks each report their own installs, spend, and self-attributed conversions. An MMP — AppsFlyer, in our case — tries to unify them, but carries its own attribution windows and fingerprinting limits. SKAN postbacks arrive twenty-four to seventy-two hours late, aggregated and privacy-preserved by design. The Play Install Referrer is deterministic on Android but limited in fields. Store consoles own ground-truth revenue but not user-level acquisition data. The internal warehouse holds in-app events, retention, and IAP — but joining it to UA data depends on a single install ID matching cleanly across systems. Ad-mediation platforms (AppLovin MAX, ironSource LevelPlay) report the ad-revenue events that actually drive ROAS in an ad-monetized portfolio like ours, and they arrive on a different cadence still.

Each source has its own definition of an install. They disagree, often by double digits. The result is a UA Manager who can open four dashboards on the same channel and see four different "true" install counts — and a chain from ad impression → click → install → in-app behavior → LTV that is broken at multiple seams. Attribution becomes guesswork. Budget allocation becomes politics. Incrementality becomes the only honest answer to "did this campaign actually cause anything?"

You can't optimize what you can't trust. The first deliverable any UA analyst should ship is the spine that makes the rest of the work honest.

The design tradeoff worth naming: the blend joins on install_id where MMP gives us one and falls back to SKAN-postback aggregates only when MMP runs materially below SKAN on iOS rows. That's deliberate — joining SKAN aggregates to user-level rows is impossible without inventing identity, and inventing identity is how double-counting starts. We accept aggregation loss on the SKAN-only top-up in exchange for never inflating an install that already exists at user-level. The trust-score weight on the top-up is what keeps a low-confidence partner from puffing up the blended count beyond what their measurement actually supports.

GIdentity stitching, honestly

The chain works in three regimes — and one of them isn't really a chain

Android. The Play Install Referrer carries a deterministic click ID and timestamp from the click that earned the install; the GAID on the device gives us a stable user identifier (until reset). The MMP consumes the referrer and writes a single install_id that ties through to in-app events, ad-revenue events, and IAP. Ad impression → click → install → in-app behavior → LTV is one row, joined cleanly. Roughly 55% of our portfolio installs sit in this regime, and they're the cohort our retention and pLTV models can be most confident about.

iOS, opted-in. When a user grants ATT consent, IDFA is available and the chain looks the same as Android — deterministic, user-level, joinable end-to-end. The catch is the consent rate: in casual card games we see roughly 25–35% opt-in, with material variance by geo and creative. So this is the highest-quality iOS data we get, but only on a quarter of the iOS cohort. We treat this slice as gold for modeling but never pretend it represents the whole iOS population — opted-in users self-select, often toward higher engagement, and projecting their behavior onto the opted-out cohort overstates LTV in predictable directions.

iOS, opted-out. SKAN. Conversion-value mapped, aggregated at the campaign × CV bucket level, delayed 24–72 hours, with no individual user identity by design. There is no clean stitch from a single ad impression to a single user's in-app behavior in this regime, and probabilistic fingerprinting — what's left of it — is shrinking under Apple's policy enforcement. The honest framing: we have aggregate signal, not a chain. Section F's blend handles this by topping up MMP with SKAN aggregates where MMP runs thin, but the top-up is a population estimate with a trust weight, not a row-level join. Anyone who tells you they have a clean per-user attribution chain on opted-out iOS is selling something.

HWhy incrementality is the honest backstop

The cleanest stitched spine still tells you correlation, not causation

Even with every join in Section F running clean, there is a question the spine cannot answer on its own: did this campaign cause this install, or would the install have happened anyway? Last-click attribution credits the platform that closed the conversion. That credit is correct accounting and incorrect causation. A user who would have organically searched Solitaire today gets credited to the Meta retargeting ad they happened to see this morning. Multiply that across every channel and the reported ROAS the spine emits is systematically too generous, in ways that vary by partner.

The dashed amber arrow in Fig. 1 is where this gets fixed. Holdout-based incrementality tests — geo holdouts, ghost-bidding, PSA tests — measure causation directly by comparing a treated population to an untreated control. The number that comes out of an honest holdout is an incremental rate: the share of attributed conversions that wouldn't have happened without the campaign. That number writes back into the trust-score weighting in Layer 2, which is what makes the loop a loop. Tab 6 walks through two of these end-to-end — a bid-strategy test on Google UAC and a geo-holdout on Meta — and the Meta result is the one that produces the discount factor the spine then applies to every Meta-attributed row going forward.

IWhat I'd ship in my first 30 days

Five concrete artifacts that turn the spine from a slide into a system

Each of the five below is a thing that exists, runs on a schedule, has an owner, and a Slack channel that gets pinged when it breaks. None of them are aspirational.

  1. Daily reconciliation report
    The Section F view runs nightly via Cloud Composer; output lands in #ua-data on Slack at 8:00 a.m. PT every weekday. Channel × OS × game rows with anchored install counts, network gaps, SKAN gaps, and any composition-shift flags from the prior day. The Moloco-style flag in Section D is one row in this report.
  2. Per-network trust-score scorecard
    Refreshed every Sunday at 9:00 p.m. PT against the prior 7 days. Reads from mw-ua.ref.network_trust_scores; the Section E grid is the human-readable face of this table. Alerts the channel owner if any of D1–D5 drops by one dot week-over-week, with the contributing rows pre-attached.
  3. SKAN postback completeness alert
    Real-time monitor on the skan_cv_received / skan_postbacks_total ratio per (channel × app × os). Fires a Slack ping when the ratio drops below 70% completeness for two consecutive days. Alert payload includes the partner AM email pre-filled and the trailing-14-day completeness trend, so the responder has the conversation half-written before they open the ticket.
  4. Creative-ID propagation audit
    One-time deep dive across all ten networks documenting which carry creative_id cleanly, which only on certain ad formats, and which not at all. Output is a maintained sheet plus a weekly automated check that flags new creative IDs appearing in spend without matching installs — that's how the Tab 4 creative analysis stops going dark on specific channels without anyone noticing.
  5. Data dictionary for fact_ua
    Every column documented: source CTE, source table, transformation logic, owner, freshness SLA. Lives as git-versioned markdown in the analytics repo, not a Notion page that decays. Anyone on UA, Creative, or Finance can answer "what does this column mean and when was it last refreshed" without pinging the data team. This is the artifact that compounds — every new analyst onboarded to the team starts here instead of in a 1:1 with someone senior.

06 · Experimentation & Incrementality

Where causation gets measured

Two experiments end-to-end. One controlled bid-strategy A/B, one geo-holdout incrementality test. The second is the one that produces the discount factor in the trust framework.

AWhy incrementality matters here

The spine reports correlation; this tab measures causation

Tab 2's Section H named the gap. Even with every join in the spine running clean, last-click attribution is correlation, not causation. The spine can tell us a Meta ad was the last touch before install — it cannot tell us the install would not have happened without that ad. Two questions our spend depends on get answered below: a controlled bid-strategy A/B (the simple case — same channel, same audience, two bid configurations) and a geo-holdout incrementality test (the harder case — measuring whether a channel's reported conversions would have happened anyway).

CPer-competitor verdicts

What we can learn · what we can ignore

One paragraph each. The Grand Harvest entry is the structural one — meta-game gap diagnosis tied to dollars; the Tripledot entry is the operational one — closest analogue, different leverage.

Solitaire Grand Harvest
Structural · meta-game gap

Grand Harvest is the case study in a structural advantage we don't have. The TriPeaks variant is wrapped in a farm-meta flywheel — players don't just complete card hands, they accumulate coins to renovate a virtual farm, which feeds back into reasons to come back tomorrow. The mechanism in plain terms: meta-game → higher D30/D60 retention → higher pLTV → higher CPI ceiling → ability to outbid in privacy-constrained auctions.

Their pLTV/CPI ratio probably lands at 1.4–1.6× (IAP-heavy economics on a meta-driven retention curve). Ours blended lands at 0.82× (Tab 5 Section D). That gap is the meta-game gap, expressed in dollars.

Closing it doesn't require us to copy farm-renovation. The recent Jigsaw events, Arcade Goal, Winning Animation Collection, and weekly tournaments are the same architectural lever applied to our codebase. The UA implication is patient — meta-features lift LTV first, which lifts the CPI ceiling next, which lets UA outbid third. This isn't a UA fix; it's a product roadmap input the UA team should advocate for.

Tripledot Studios
Operational · closest analogue

Tripledot is the structurally closest competitor to us — classic mechanics, strong UA capability, ad-plus-IAP monetization, multi-title portfolio. What they do better today: faster creative iteration cadence (closer to weekly than our biweekly), broader channel diversification (active across Moloco's bidding tier where our spend is still ramping), and a UA-first organizational culture where channel managers drive product feature requests, not the other way around.

What we have that they don't: 35 years of brand authority, the original mobile Solitaire (100M+ player history), and portfolio cross-promotion inventory we're under-using (Tab 8 Brief 5 quantifies the gap at 12–18% category benchmark vs our 3.4%).

The right framing: we're not behind a different company; we're a parallel company with different leverage. The Tab 8 SKAN-optimized creative engine and the 90-day plan close the iteration-cadence gap. The cross-title synergy initiative monetizes the inventory advantage Tripledot can't replicate.

Solitaire Cash & Solitaire Smash
RMG · different beast

RMG titles run under a different regulatory and UA economy — age-gated, geo-restricted (currently 30-some US states plus a handful of international markets), with their own MMP handling for chargebacks and refunds, and a fraud surface that drives up tracking complexity and CPI. Their reported $15–20M monthly UA spend isn't directly comparable to ours: a real-money skill competition produces 5–7× the revenue per active user that an ad-monetized casual game does, which raises the CPI ceiling without raising the LTV multiple in any way we can copy.

We can learn their hooks — countdown urgency, leaderboard FOMO, real-stakes language adapted to non-cash equivalents like daily tournaments and cosmetic prizes. We cannot learn their unit economics. The right read on RMG is one paragraph long; entire competitive decks have been written treating these games as comparable, and they aren't.

Microsoft Solitaire Collection
Organic competitor

Microsoft's strategy is brand authority plus ecosystem distribution. They spend almost nothing on UA (under $1M/month) and capture roughly 5M monthly installs largely organic — App Store and Play Store search visibility plus Windows preinstall plus Xbox cross-promotion. D7 retention is reportedly high; Microsoft account login increases stickiness in a way third-party titles can't replicate.

Read: they're an organic competitor, not a UA competitor. The lesson is in their daily-challenge cadence and the simplicity of their always-available offline mode — both already informing our product roadmap. Where we compete with them isn't on the App Store search page; it's on the difference between "Solitaire" the brand category and "MobilityWare Solitaire" the product. ASO and store conversion-rate optimization matter here more than channel mix.

Brainium
Quality benchmark

Brainium is the clean-execution competitor. Limited UA spend ($2–3M/month), strong organic share via App Store reviews (their review count and rating consistently lead the casual-card category), and creative that prioritizes gameplay clarity over hook intensity. The lesson: review velocity matters as a long-term UA input we under-invest in. Their unit economics on the ad-monetized side are likely close to ours, slightly favoring them on the App Store conversion funnel. Worth watching as the benchmark for what a high-quality classic experience without a meta-layer looks like at scale.

01 / 05 LTV lever

High-Value User Lookalike Loop

Shift acquisition mix toward higher-LTV users by seeding lookalikes from our top-5%-spender cohort across Meta, Moloco, ASA Custom Product Pages, and TikTok.

Insight
The top 5% of US iOS players generate roughly 20% of global gaming revenue (Moloco, 2025). On our spine, that 5% maps to a specific cohort: D30 retention 3.6× the portfolio average, ad-revenue per user 2.1×, IAP penetration 7×. We have the feature set to seed lookalikes against this cohort — we've never wired the loop.
Hypothesis
Acquisition campaigns seeded with first-party lookalikes of our top-5% spenders will deliver D30 pLTV at least 35% above blended portfolio average, even at a CPI premium of 25–40%. The economics work because the LTV gap is bigger than the CPI gap.
Target audience
Lookalike audiences seeded from US/CA/UK/AU iOS top-5%-spender cohorts in Solitaire and TriPeaks (the two titles with mature LTV signal). 1% lookalikes for precision, 3% lookalikes for volume.
Channel mix
Meta (CAPI custom audience uploads, 1% and 3% lookalikes), Moloco (1P audience seeding via direct-deal lane), ASA Custom Product Pages (per-cohort variants), TikTok (1P upload + Pixel for retargeting). Excluded: Google UAC — its black-box audience model dilutes custom seeds in the bid lattice.
Creative direction
Three concept tracks. (1) Mastery — leaning into win-streaks and progression cues, the strongest behavior signal in top-5% retention. (2) Daily ritual — top-5% players play 6.2 days per week vs. 2.4 portfolio average. (3) High-stakes moments — late-game scenarios, not tutorial gameplay. All three avoid the "free coins" hook entirely; it over-indexes for low-LTV reward-seekers.
Budget & timeline
$220K over 8 weeks. Weeks 1–2 audience build + creative production. Weeks 3–6 spend ramp ($30K/wk → $60K/wk). Weeks 7–8 readout. Split: $90K Meta · $60K Moloco · $40K ASA CPP · $30K TikTok.
KPI & guardrails
Primary: D30 pLTV (lookalike cohort) ≥ 1.35× portfolio average. Guardrails: D7 retention ≥ 11% (vs. 12% portfolio); install volume ≥ 6,000 in test window; CPI ≤ 1.45× portfolio blended average. Secondary: D60 ad-revenue-per-installer.
Test design
Treatment: lookalike-seeded campaigns. Control: matched same-channel same-creative campaigns running broad targeting on the same titles, geos, window. Bayesian readout alongside frequentist — posterior P(treatment LTV ≥ 1.35× control) > 0.85 is the bar. Statistical readout end of week 6 once D30 cohort matures.
Risks
(1) Lookalike-1% audiences shrink fast; mitigate with 3% pools for volume. (2) Moloco's direct-deal seeding is the channel currently flagged in Tab 2 Section D — hold its allocation at $30K until the SDK regression resolves. (3) iOS post-ATT signal loss may undercount D30 LTV on the opted-out cohort; cross-check against the SKAN CV-bucket distribution computed by the Section F view.
02 / 05 Measurement lever

SKAN-Optimized Creative Iteration Engine

A two-week creative-test cadence purpose-built for post-ATT measurement, with a CV-mapping that makes signal legible even when probabilistic data is sparse.

Insight
Tab 2 Section E shows D4 (creative-ID propagation) is the weakest dimension across half our ad networks — Meta scores 1/5, UAC 2/5. Without creative-level granularity on opted-out iOS, conventional A/B creative testing fails silently. The fix isn't more impressions; it's a conversion-value mapping that makes a 14-day SKAN window readable by creative variant.
Hypothesis
A 6-bucket CV mapping aligned to D0–D7 in-app events (level-5 reached, 3rd session, ad-impression-ARPU > $0.10, etc.) will let us read creative winners at 95% confidence on iOS within 14 days, vs. the current 4-week-or-never status. Tested winners will produce a 6-month CTR-decay slope at least 20% slower than the portfolio creative average.
Target audience
All paid iOS traffic across Solitaire, Spider Solitaire, TriPeaks, Bubble Shooter Pop. Android cohort runs as a deterministic check (probabilistic creative attribution still works there) — Android winners predict iOS winners ~70% of the time, and that's the model we'll use to triage iOS variants faster.
Channel mix
Networks where D4 ≥ 4/5 in our framework — AppLovin, Moloco, InMobi, Liftoff, ASA. Excluded from this engine: Meta (1/5 propagation, runs separate AAA workflow) and UAC (2/5, separate experimentation track). Phase-2 expansion to Unity Ads (3/5) once we earn creative-ID parity from their team.
Creative direction
Every two weeks, ship a 4-variant test on each (title × network × OS) cell. Variants must vary on exactly one dimension — hook (0–3s), pacing, CTA, or audio. Ban "one-arm" creative changes; that's how we get unreadable tests. Keep a permanent control creative for cross-test calibration. Each creative spends 14 days live, then retires regardless — fatigue dominates after that (Tab 4 fatigue curve).
Budget & timeline
$30K/cell/cycle × ~12 active cells = $360K/cycle, ongoing. First cycle weeks 1–3, second cycle weeks 4–6, etc. CV mapping is a 2-week up-front engineering investment that pays off cycle-after-cycle.
KPI & guardrails
Primary: 95%-confidence creative-winner identification within 14 days on ≥ 60% of (title × network × iOS) cells. Guardrails: blended CPI not up > 5%; D7 retention parity within 1pp of pre-engine baseline; iteration cycle time dropping not increasing. Secondary: 6-month CTR-decay slope improvement on tested winners.
Test design
This is the test infrastructure, not a single test. The validation experiment is a meta-test: run the engine on Solitaire iOS for 6 weeks; compare creative-winner rank-correlation between the SKAN-readable cycle and the deterministic Android cohort cycle. If they're aligned (Spearman ρ > 0.6), the engine works. If not, the CV mapping needs revision.
Risks
(1) CV mapping is a one-shot bet per app — wrong mapping costs months. Mitigate by piloting on Solitaire only first, validating against Android, then porting. (2) SKAN postback-completeness drops will read as creative regressions (false signals); the Tab 2 Section I postback alert needs to be live before launch. (3) Creative producers will resist the 14-day rotation cap. Frame it as "we're paying for the 14 best days, not the slow tail" and bring them into the design.
03 / 05 Retention lever

Re-Engagement Engine for Dormant High-D7 Cohorts

Identify users who showed strong D1–D7 engagement but churned by D30; build a SKAN-aware retargeting playbook paired with in-app event hooks tied to live ops.

Insight
Our D30 retention cliff sits between D7 and D14. ~62% of users who hit D7 retention churn within the next 7 days — and within that group, ~40% never see a live-ops event because the live-ops cadence kicks in at week 3. We're losing a high-engagement cohort to a content gap we control.
Hypothesis
Retargeting the dormant-D7-strong cohort with a campaign timed to a specific live-ops event will produce ≥ 18% reactivation rate within 14 days of touch, and reactivated users will show D60 retention parity with the never-churned D30 cohort within ±15%. Implication: re-engagement here isn't a discounted UA channel — it's a cheaper-than-acquisition way to add a known-good user.
Target audience
Users with D1 retention TRUE, D7 retention TRUE, last_session NULL for 14–21 days. Cohort scope: Solitaire iOS US/CA/UK and TriPeaks Android US — the two cells with cleanest in-app event tracking. Estimated cohort size: ~340K users on a rolling 30-day basis.
Channel mix
Meta (CAPI custom audience), Moloco (direct-deal user-list seeding), TikTok (Pixel-based retargeting). Push notifications + email run as a parallel zero-cost channel and serve as a control arm — paid channels need to outperform owned-media reactivation by enough to justify the spend.
Creative direction
Three tracks tied to the live-ops calendar. (1) Tournament returning — when a Solitaire weekly tournament launches, message dormant users 24h before. (2) Streak rescue — for users with a documented win-streak history, message at the day-mark when their streak would have continued. (3) New jigsaw event — Solitaire's recent jigsaw rollout has higher D1-back rates than any other live-op; lean in. Avoid generic "we miss you" copy — it tests poorly across the casual-card category.
Budget & timeline
$140K over 12 weeks. Three 4-week test waves, each tied to a different live-ops cycle: Solitaire tournament (week 1), TriPeaks event (week 5), Solitaire jigsaw (week 9). Split: $70K Meta · $40K Moloco · $30K TikTok.
KPI & guardrails
Primary: 14-day reactivation rate ≥ 18% on paid arms vs. push/email control. Secondary: D60 retention parity with never-churned cohort within ±15%. Guardrails: D14 ad-ARPU on reactivated cohort ≥ pre-churn ad-ARPU (otherwise the reactivated user isn't worth the spend); cost-per-reactivation ≤ 0.5× current iOS US blended CPI.
Test design
User-level holdout: 80/20 treatment/control within the dormant cohort. Treatment receives paid retargeting; control receives only push/email. Compare 14-day reactivation, then D60 retention, then D60 ad-revenue. Bayesian readout — posterior P(treatment reactivation ≥ control + 6pp) > 0.9 is the bar. User-level randomization at the AppsFlyer audience layer is non-negotiable; campaign-level randomization invalidates the readout.
Risks
(1) iOS opt-out cohort can't be retargeted on Meta-by-IDFA — rely on email-hash custom audiences where consented at signup. (2) Live-ops event cadence is owned by Product; campaigns timed to events that get pushed/canceled are dead spend. Lock launch dates with Product before booking media. (3) Reactivation campaigns can poach users who would have come back organically — the holdout structure controls for this, which is exactly why the user-level randomization point above isn't optional.
04 / 05 Expansion lever

Emerging Markets Expansion Sprint — BR, MX, ID, PH

Six-week pilot across four high-growth emerging markets, scoped from day one with a geo-holdout incrementality structure rather than a self-attribution bet.

Insight
Top 1% iOS bid prices rose 140% in 2024 (Moloco). T1 markets are getting structurally more expensive every quarter, while iOS share in Brazil, Mexico, Indonesia, and the Philippines is climbing — and casual-card retention in these markets is within 8% of T1 averages on the data we already have. The bid-pressure delta is real; the retention delta isn't.
Hypothesis
Solitaire iOS in BR/MX and Solitaire Android in ID/PH will deliver D30 ROAS within 20% of US/UK at less than 60% of US/UK CPI. Even if D30 ROAS is somewhat softer, blended portfolio CPI drops materially because the marginal CPI savings translate to incremental scaled installs.
Target audience
BR: iOS 18–44, urban. MX: iOS 18–44, T1+T2 cities. ID: Android, all-SES (Android device-price spread is wide). PH: Android 18–44. All campaigns scoped to local-language creative — generic English creative is the #1 expansion-failure mode in casual card.
Channel mix
Google UAC (broad reach in EM), Moloco (strong BR/MX inventory mix), TikTok (massive scale in ID/PH), AppLovin (strong Android performance), ASA (BR + MX only — App Store presence in ID/PH is much smaller than Google Play). Excluded for this sprint: Meta and Unity — too expensive a learning curve to run alongside a tight 6-week cycle.
Creative direction
Localized hooks, not translated hooks. (1) BR — "jogo offline" (offline play) tests strongly; Brazilians flag mobile-data sensitivity in casual gaming. (2) MX — family-friendly framing, "jugar con la familia" angles. (3) ID/PH — short-session emphasis (1–3 minute play windows); light-mode visuals for low-end Android screens. Native-language voiceover on video formats, not subtitles.
Budget & timeline
$180K over 6 weeks. Week 1: localization + baseline data pulls. Weeks 2–5: spend ramp. Week 6: readout. Per-market split: $50K BR · $50K MX · $40K ID · $40K PH. Reserve: $0 — emerging-markets pilots fail by being half-funded; commit fully or don't run.
KPI & guardrails
Primary: D30 ROAS ≥ 0.8× US/UK D30 ROAS in matching titles. Guardrails: D7 retention not below 9% (lower edge of portfolio range); blended CPI ≤ $1.20 in BR/MX iOS and ≤ $0.60 in ID/PH Android. Secondary: ad-ARPU per installer ≥ 0.7× T1 — if below 0.5×, reconsider entry.
Test design
Geo-holdout incrementality from day one. Within each pilot market, 4 high-population DMAs as treatment, 4 as control (paid spend held off in control DMAs for the full 6 weeks). Pre-period parallel-trends check on organic install volume in weeks −3 to −1 to validate matching. Read incremental rate at week 6; compare to network-attributed install count to set the discount factor going forward — this is a Tab 2 feedback-loop write.
Risks
(1) EM ad-revenue eCPMs run 30–50% lower than T1, which compresses pLTV — model it correctly upfront, don't pretend T1 monetization translates. (2) Currency volatility (BRL, MXN, IDR, PHP) will distort week-over-week ROAS if not USD-anchored at spend time; the Section F view does USD-conversion at point-of-spend, lean on it. (3) Local-language creative production at speed risks tone-deafness; budget includes a 1-week native-speaker QA loop before launch — non-negotiable.
05 / 05 Organic lever · the inside game

Cross-Title Portfolio Synergy

Formalize cross-promotion between Solitaire ↔ Spider Solitaire ↔ TriPeaks ↔ Bubble Shooter Pop, treating the portfolio as our largest organic UA channel — and tie it back to the data-fragmentation problem the workbench was built to solve.

Insight
Our flagship Solitaire app has reached ~100M global players. The cross-title overlap visible in our internal warehouse — users with installs across two of our games — sits at ~3.4%. Industry benchmarks for studios that actively cross-promote run 12–18%. We're sitting on the largest piece of paid-equivalent UA inventory in the portfolio and not using it.
Hypothesis
A formalized cross-promotion engine produces ≥ 9% lift in second-title installs from the existing Solitaire active-user base, at effectively zero incremental media cost (the inventory is our own ad slots). Second-title users acquired via cross-promotion show D30 retention at par with paid-acquired users — and likely 20–40% higher, because demonstrated genre-affinity is a stronger signal than any algorithmic targeting.
Target audience
Solitaire DAU as the seed cohort; promotion targeting depends on title pair. Solitaire → Spider Solitaire (high genre overlap). Solitaire → TriPeaks (lateral casual move). Solitaire → Bubble Shooter Pop (broader casual cross-sell). All four titles seeded back to each other in the inverse direction.
Channel mix
Owned-and-operated entirely. In-app cross-promo ad slots, end-of-game-state interstitials, app-launch promo carousels, and a coordinated push-notification calendar for cross-title event launches. No paid media in this initiative — that's the point.
Creative direction
Treat cross-promo creative with the same rigor as paid creative: A/B-tested, fatigue-tracked, refresh cadence locked. Common failure mode: treating cross-promo as "free" and shipping low-effort creative — that wastes the inventory and trains users to ignore it. Three tracks: (1) genre-bridge ("Loved Solitaire? Try Spider"); (2) live-ops cross-pull (Solitaire jigsaw → TriPeaks Pyramid event); (3) achievement-bridge (your Solitaire streak → TriPeaks first-day reward).
Budget & timeline
$25K total — for instrumentation, creative production, and analytics tooling. No media spend. 8-week build, ongoing operation. Weeks 1–3: cross-title attribution layer build (the hard part). Weeks 4–5: creative production. Weeks 6–7: pilot across two title pairs. Week 8: readout, then scale to full portfolio.
KPI & guardrails
Primary: ≥ 9% second-title install lift on seeded title pairs by week 6, vs. unseeded baseline. Secondary: D30 retention parity with paid-acquired cohort within ±15%. Guardrails: primary-title D7 retention not down (cross-promo can't eat into the host title's engagement); cross-promo ad-slot replacement-cost ≤ $40K/quarter portfolio-wide (those slots currently sell to third-party networks).
Test design
User-level randomized holdout within Solitaire DAU: 50% see cross-promo creative, 50% see standard third-party ad inventory. Compare second-title install rate, primary-title D7 retention, and primary-title ad-revenue between arms. Run for 6 weeks to capture D30 retention readout on the second-title cohort.
Risks
(1) The cross-title attribution layer is itself a Tab 2 fragmentation problem. One player playing two of our games is a single user across two install_id streams that don't currently share a join key. Solving this is the harder half of this initiative — and it's also the foundation for portfolio-level pLTV modeling. (2) Cross-promo slots currently produce ~$160K/quarter in third-party ad revenue across the portfolio; this initiative trades some of that for second-title installs. The math has to be done carefully — at what cross-promo conversion rate does the trade clear? Build the model before launching. (3) Primary-title cannibalization is the cardinal risk: if Solitaire DAU spend less time on Solitaire because they're now playing TriPeaks, the primary-title ad-revenue hit could exceed the second-title lift. The user-level holdout is what tells us whether this is happening.

Five briefs, five levers. The thing they share is the spine. None of them work without the trust framework, the reconciliation, and the feedback loop in Tab 2 — which is why Tab 2 is built first.
